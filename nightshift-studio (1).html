The Boatman's Lamp

Short story · 2 min read · Written at the studio

Every night the old boatman hung a lamp at the front of his boat, though nobody had crossed the river after dark in years. When the village children asked why, he said the lamp was not for people. It was for the river, which got lonely when the ferries stopped.

The children laughed, and the boatman let them. He trimmed the wick, wiped the glass, and sat with his feet over the water while the current talked to itself in low, wet syllables.

The monsoon came early that year. For eleven nights the rain was so thick the lamp lit only a small gold room around the boat, and he sat inside it anyway, because that was the job he had given himself.

On the twelfth night the rain thinned. Far off, on the opposite bank, a second light blinked once, twice, and held steady.

He never learned whose it was. He hung his own lamp a little higher after that.
